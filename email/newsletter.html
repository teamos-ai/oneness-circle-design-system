Your cells don’t just need nutrients. They need information.

Rachel Mense   ·   20 August 2026

  ◆  

Most of what we’re offered for energy is a version of the same idea: give the body more of something. More protein, more magnesium, more of whatever the current answer is.

It’s a reasonable idea. It’s also only half of one.

Supplying a cell with raw material is a different question from whether that cell knows what to do with it.

The turn

Every second, trillions of cells are sending and receiving signals — coordinating repair, deciding what to prioritise, responding to what’s happening around them. That conversation is happening a long way upstream of anything you’d notice, and it isn’t a supply problem. It’s a communication one.

This is why two people can eat almost identically and feel entirely different. The inputs were the same. What the body did with them wasn’t.

◆ ◆ ◆

Where this breaks down

I want to be careful here, because this is exactly the point where wellness writing usually oversells. I can’t tell you that any particular thing fixes cellular signalling — I don’t know that, and anyone who tells you they do is guessing in a confident voice. What I can say is that it’s a more interesting question than the one we usually ask, and that the things which seem to support it are unglamorous: sleep, movement, light, what you eat, how much you’re carrying.

The small thing

Tomorrow morning, before you reach for your phone, go outside for two minutes. That’s it. Not a protocol. Just early light, which is one of the oldest signals your body knows how to read.

I’ll write again next week.

— Rachel
